# Pricing Packaging — Charge for the Outcome

Turn an approved offer into tiers anchored to buyer ROI — before any outbound or sales call quotes a number.

No outbound-prospecting, no closing-deals quotes until tiers are approved.

## Prerequisites

An approved designing-offer sheet. If none exists, invoke that skill first. Never price a promise you haven't written down.

## The Pricing Card

Produce this artifact and get approval:

1. **ROI anchor.** The buyer's money math: what the outcome is worth per month/year (hours saved × cost, revenue recovered, deals won). Show the calculation. Price lives or dies here.
2. **Three tiers.** Good / Better / Best with one clear upgrade reason each:
   - **Starter** — outcome for one unit, slowest speed, buyer does homework.
   - **Core (recommended)** — full outcome, fastest reasonable speed, done-with-you or done-for-you.
   - **Premium** — outcome plus insurance: speed, access, guarantee extension.
3. **Price rule.** Core tier = 10-20% of first-year ROI value. Starter ≈ 50% of Core. Premium ≈ 2× Core. If the math breaks, the offer — not the price — is wrong.
4. **Payment terms.** Upfront vs milestones vs recurring, stated plainly. Default: 50% upfront, 50% on delivery for projects; monthly for retainers. State the default and the condition that breaks it.
5. **Raise trigger.** What happens that raises prices (3 wins at this price, waitlist >2 weeks, close rate >40%). Prices ratchet one way until evidence says otherwise.

## Guardrails

- **Frozen tiers, one floor.** The approved card is the only quotable price. Starter is the floor — nothing quotes below it.
- **No discount without a trade.** Every concession takes scope, speed, or terms back. Concession without trade is logged as a policy break, not a tactic.
- **No ad-hoc per-buyer pricing.** Custom numbers need a written reason + expiry date, reviewed monthly. Two custom deals in a row means the card is wrong — fix the card.
- **No quoting before the card exists.** A price said on a call before approval is a verbal commitment you now own. Card first, calls second.

## Questions That Matter

Ask at most 3, one round, then draft:

1. What is the outcome worth to the buyer in money per year?
2. What have they paid for adjacent solutions before?
3. What tier do you want 60% of buyers to pick, and why?

## Anti-Patterns

- **Hourly pricing.** Hours punish speed and cap income. Price the outcome.
- **One price.** One price forces a yes/no. Three tiers let buyers choose how to say yes.
- **Discounting on the call.** Discounts without trade (scope, speed, terms) teach buyers to haggle. Trade, don't concede.
- **Hiding the price.** If buyers must call to learn the range, unqualified calls eat your week. Publish the range early.

## Done Looks Like

An approved pricing card: ROI math shown, three tiers with upgrade reasons, payment terms, and a raise trigger. Next skills: outbound-prospecting (fill the pipeline) or closing-deals (quote on calls).
